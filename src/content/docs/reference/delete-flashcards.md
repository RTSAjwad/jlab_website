---
title: Delete Your Flashcards to Improve
description: Why keeping old flashcards is a hidden trap, and how deleting cards improves progress.
---

**Summary**: Keeping old flashcards is a hidden trap — language learning requires
adapting to new contexts, but Anki keeps you stuck in the same ones. This
silently limits your progress. Deleting cards is key.

Anki does not account for a crucial aspect of language learning: the constant
switching of context. Consider the word "bank" in these sentences:

- "I deposited my paycheck at the bank."
- "We picnicked by the river bank."

The sentence context not only defines the meaning of "bank" but also makes this
word easier to remember. This is why many learners use full sentences in their
flashcards. However, this comes at a cost: your understanding of a word becomes
tied to a specific sentence, making it harder to recognize in new contexts.

Native speakers effortlessly adapt to shifting contexts, but learners using
flashcards may struggle because their knowledge is forever bound to static
examples. If the same word always appears in the same sentence, it reinforces a
rigid association instead of real linguistic flexibility.

This leads to the following pattern, with negative consequences:

- The context of a sentence makes learning easier. But once familiar, the
  flashcard loses its learning value and should be removed.
- Anki's strong reputation makes learners afraid to delete cards, fearing they'll
  lose knowledge.
- They repeat useless, old cards and feel productive (because they remember
  sentences quickly) while actually wasting time.

The solution is simple: **delete (or suspend) them!** Suspending keeps cards in
Anki but they are never displayed again until unsuspended. **For the Jlab decks,
suspending might be better than deleting, because updates would add deleted cards
again.** When mining sentences from subs2srs decks, cards were always deleted.

## Thoughts to make this easier

- Deleted cards were never missed, and none were ever restored.
- For a new word to really stick, it needs to be seen in 2-5 _different_
  sentences. You do not need to keep the first one.
- It is ok to look up a word multiple times. Keeping it on a single flashcard
  forever rarely helps.
- A language is a spaced repetition system itself — words and grammar repeat
  themselves.
- You have to get used to the context words appear in. This is crucial for
  listening comprehension.
- It is ok to forget a few things.
- Deleting reduces review load and prevents burnout and boredom.
- Deleting makes you faster.

## When to delete

As an intermediate learner, cards were deleted after seeing them after an
interval of **~12-20 days** (kept for ~1 month in total). For beginners, keep
them a bit longer. In Anki, the time since the last repetition roughly
corresponds to what's on the "hard" button:

![The hard button hint](/assets/Delete-hint-hard.jpg)

Deleting cards is much more important than worrying about the settings.

## How to delete

- Move old cards to a separate deck and export it out of Anki with scheduling
  information (filter by interval in the card browser). This way they could be
  restored — though none were ever imported again.
- In AnkiDroid, set up a swipe-up action: when the hard button showed 12-20 days,
  swipe up to remove it.
- The Jlab addon has built-in card management.
