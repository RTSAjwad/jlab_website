## Questions about learning in general

### Which anime should I use?

The first anime used for studying were: Isshuukan Friends, My Neighbor Totoro,
Shirokuma Cafe and Natsume's Book of Friends. Especially the first two are fairly
easy, but this does not mean you will understand them right away. After those,
whatever was interesting was used.

In general, it's recommended to start with slice of life, as it teaches less foul
language than e.g. fighting. The difficulty doesn't matter too much if you use
the right mining strategy: absolutely make sure to only use easy parts for your
active studies and skip everything that is too hard.

### How much will I understand after the beginner's course?

Unfortunately, the honest answer is: you will understand very little. After
learning the basics, it took thoroughly going through around 250 episodes of
anime and dorama, mining around 15k more flashcards, and throughout the whole
process there was always a lot that could not be understood right away.

Decent fluency in listening comprehension takes a huge effort, but it's worth it.

### How to google for grammar

In many cases it is easy to google for the meaning of _given_ grammar. This is
very helpful when studying with anime and often faster than asking a question
online. See the [dedicated article](/jlab_website/reference/googling-grammar/).

### Card image triggers your memory

Seeing the image on the front of the card can remind you of the sentence's
meaning without actually reading or listening to it. Similarly, hearing the first
word(s) of the audio can trigger the full sentence. However, the goal is to
understand the sentence in Japanese, not just recall it from these cues.
Solutions:

- Move the image to the card's back — see
  [this article](/jlab_website/addon/card-customization/).
- Use the meaning for output practice (try to recall a few words or sentence
  fragments).

### How to practice speaking

A good experience came from casual, "sloppy" shadowing while doing listening
cards. Dedicated output flashcards are not recommended. See
[the article](/jlab_website/guides/speaking/).

### Why should I suspend/delete flashcards?!

They block your progress at some point — see
[this article](/jlab_website/reference/delete-flashcards/).

## Deck-specific questions

### What are the cloze / reading cards for?

The cloze/reading practice deck is a product of personal studies with subs2srs
decks. It was used to practice English-to-Japanese with cloze deletions (a kind
of fill-in-the-blank exercise). The Jlab addon automatically generates these
clozes when looking up words in the listening deck.

The beginner's course does not include any cloze deletions by default. In this
case, you can use the deck to practice kana. Completing this deck is not
mandatory.

Even though the cloze cards read "audio is not played automatically", the audio
DOES play sometimes. This can be changed in the deck options.

### Correct order for intermediate decks

The intermediate decks build on each other and should be done in order. For
example, intermediate 1 teaches words that are used in intermediate 2.

### Which subs2srs decks are usable

The deck collection is a huge mess — neither the previous owner nor Jlab took the
time to sort out broken decks. For mining cards for the Jlab decks, broken ones
were sorted out locally. There is a list of deck names for finding a good one.

## Technical questions

### Card Customization

Cards in Anki can be customized to a great extent — for example, move the image
to the back or remove the latin characters on all cards at once. See
[the introduction](/jlab_website/addon/card-customization/).

### Anki automatically plays my audio

Change this in the deck options (not the general settings): it's called "don't
play audio automatically".

### Create English → Japanese vocab cards

Before going ahead, check out [how to practice output](/jlab_website/guides/speaking/). If you
still want to turn vocab cards into English → Japanese, you can exchange the
front/back templates. If you want both Japanese → English and English → Japanese,
add a second card type and exchange front/back templates on that type.
