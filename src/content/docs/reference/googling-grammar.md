---
title: How to Google for Japanese Grammar
description: A search technique for quickly finding the meaning of Japanese grammar using hiragana.
---

**Summary**: If you google for "Japanese grammar" (spelled in latin characters)
plus the grammar term in hiragana, the solution to your grammar problems is
usually among the first 1-5 hits. This works amazingly well even for tricky
questions.

This search technique made learning Japanese much easier, often reducing the
solution to understanding given grammar to just a quick Google search. It is not
suitable for complete beginners — you should be able to distinguish words,
sentence parts, and a bit of grammar.

- Try to break down a sentence into chunks and isolate the smallest part you
  don't understand.
- Google for the hiragana part of this, e.g. `japanese grammar として`.
- Putting the hiragana part in quotation marks enforces its occurrence in the
  search results (but try without those first).
- Play around with variations of the hiragana part. Add additional guide words
  (spelled in latin characters, e.g. "verb", "adjective", "grammar" or
  "difference").
- As a rule of thumb, the relevant part is usually not written in kanji. The
  hiragana part, even if incomplete or wrong, often takes you to the solution.

If googling does not help, you can ask the question (with a full sentence) on
HiNative or Reddit — but be very precise about the part you do not understand,
otherwise you will just get a translation. Do NOT trust the AI-generated answers
on HiNative.

## Examples

### "Don't you know this egg?"

![Egg scene](/jlab_website/assets/tamago.jpg)

> そこ の 巣 に あった 卵 を 知らぬ か
> *(soko no su ni atta tamago wo shiranu ka — "Don't you know the egg in this nest?")*

- 巣 **su**: nest
- 卵 **tamago**: egg
- 知る **shiru**: to know

The sentence is from Natsume's Book of Friends. Originally, 知らぬ (shiranu, an
uncommon verb negation not found in every textbook) was the source of confusion.

- **Search 1**: Omit the kanji, google for `japanese grammar らぬ` — first hit.
  Not even the correct grammar term, because "ら" is part of the negative verb
  stem.
- **Search 2**: `japanese grammar ぬ` (the correct ending in this case) — first
  hit.

### "I have been forced to stop"

<audio controls src="/jlab_website/assets/Ongaku.mp3">Your browser does not support the audio element.</audio>

![Music scene](/jlab_website/assets/Ongaku.jpg)

> 私 は あなた に 音楽 辞めさせられた ん だ よ
> *(watashi ha, anata ni ongaku yamesaserareta n da yo — "I've been forced to quit playing music by you")*

辞めさせられた (yamesaserareta) is a complex conjugation: past passive causative
of yameru (to stop).

- `japanese grammar めさせられた` reveals a nice YouTube video — but this has the
  verb stem inside (me from yameru).
- `japanese grammar させられた` is the correct grammar term.

### "Don't come closer"

<audio controls src="/jlab_website/assets/kuruna.mp3">Your browser does not support the audio element.</audio>

![Don't come closer scene](/jlab_website/assets/kuruna2.jpg)

> 来る な 殺される ぞ
> *(kuru na korosareru zo — "don't come (closer), you'll be killed")*

The "na" here is the negative command form (kuru na — don't come). Since it's
quite strong, it's maybe not taught in class. Even though the kana part "るな"
doesn't accurately describe this grammar, `japanese grammar るな` brings up the
solution in the first hit. Since we know "na" is related to a verb here, try
`japanese grammar な verb`.

### "Get out of here"

<audio controls src="/jlab_website/assets/takunakattara.mp3">Your browser does not support the audio element.</audio>

![Get out of here scene](/jlab_website/assets/takunakattara.jpg)

> 死にたくなかったら 早く ここ から 逃げ出しなさい
> *(shinitakunakattara hayaku koko kara nigedashinasai — "if you don't want to die, get out of here quickly")*

shitakunakattara is another conjugation chain: shinu (to die) → shinitakunai →
shinitakunakatta → shinitakunakattara.

- The naive kana-only query `japanese grammar にたくなかったら` takes you to one
  part of the chain (takunakatta) only. This reveals that "ni" is part of the
  verb stem.
- Omit "ni" and put the rest in quotation marks: `japanese grammar
  "たくなかったら"`. The first and third hits show translations.
- Googling for different versions, e.g. `japanese grammar "なかったら"` or
  `japanese grammar "かったら"`, take you to the potential form.
