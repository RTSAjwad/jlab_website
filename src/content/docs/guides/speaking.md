A common question is: **"How should I practice speaking?"** The recommendation:
do not create separate output flashcards, but try casual shadowing while doing
the listening cards. If you want to put more focus on speaking, follow Jeff
Brown's method.

Speaking skills aren't perfect here from rarely practicing conversations —
however, it's possible to get along in daily life entirely without talking to
someone. Shadowing is what was done most of the time, developing a habit called
_sloppy shadowing_.

## Sloppy Shadowing

Shadowing means to revoice or recite given content and is a common tool in
language learning. By _sloppy_ shadowing, we mean you should not apply any
perfectionism here: you do not have to repeat a full sentence — words or parts
of the sentence are just fine. This perfectly aligns with the general
recommendation of keeping the learning curve flat, and is the complement to the
always-praised "comprehensible input": casual, easy output that does not
overwhelm you.

Sloppy shadowing has four stages, ideally done while practicing listening
comprehension:

1. **Repeat words**: Begin by shadowing individual words from a given sentence.
2. **Move to sentence parts**: Once comfortable, start shadowing parts of
   sentences. Try different parts — over time you'll get a good feeling for what
   works.
3. **Full sentences**: Shadow complete sentences.
4. **Memorize and shadow full scripts**: When you've been able to shadow full
   sentences for a while, you can try full scripts. Don't do this too early and
   remember that parts of this are fine! The video recommendations' transcripts
   can help. Note that "memorize" does not mean long-term memorization.

### Rules to stick to

- Output is the hardest part of language learning, so do not feel frustrated if
  it does not work right away.
- No perfectionism — you do not need to repeat a full sentence. Parts are just
  fine.
- If something is too hard to shadow, don't do it. You'll find something easier
  soon.
- Never use only text — always use something with audio and pay careful
  attention to correct pronunciation. It's easy to form bad habits, but they
  wear off over time.
- Do not shadow everything you encounter. Stick to what you like.
- It's ok to forget sentences, you will still move forward. Think about your
  mother tongue: how many things can you fully recite?
- A good time to shadow when doing flashcards is when the card's meaning is tied
  to its image or the first words. Try to voice fragments of the sentence then.
- Instead of repeating a large part once, it's better to repeat a smaller and
  easier part multiple times.
- You will not improve overnight; it will take long to become fluent.
- For longer audio, transcriptions help, but make sure to listen to the audio
  first.

## Jeffery Brown's Method

If shadowing is not enough and you want more output early, check out what Jeff
Brown (professor of linguistics) did to learn Arabic. All details are in an
excellent 1-hour [YouTube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=illApgaLgGA). Two key points to
consider:

- Many in the language learning community discourage early output, fearing it
  could lead to the manifestation of mistakes. There is some agreement with
  this, but Jeff impressively demonstrates that early output can be effective.
  The key is to maintain a mindset of continuous improvement and the will to get
  rid of possible mistakes.
- You need the right partner or teacher for this. What to do: write down exactly
  what Jeff did and deduce rules for your tandem partner.
